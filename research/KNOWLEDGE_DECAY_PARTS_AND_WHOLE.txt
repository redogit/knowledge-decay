KNOWLEDGE DECAY: IDENTITY, RELATIONSHIPS, AND LEARNING
Public research summary
Status: scoped synthesis of recovered research; not a new proof audit.

1. PURPOSE
Knowledge Decay investigates how people can develop new understanding from
surviving evidence, alternative explanations, and practical tests. Information
structures support this inquiry by retaining context, uncertainty, and the means
to revisit earlier reasoning. Complete historical reconstruction is not assumed.

2. RESEARCH QUESTION
How can a record maintain a reliable, revisable account of its identity,
dependencies, relationship to a larger system, and unresolved questions?

Shared context may mean identical information, equal access to an overview, or
role-specific views. These alternatives are not equivalent. A common system
revision with explicit relationships is one candidate design, not a settled rule.

3. CONNECTIONS TO EARLIER WORK

Part-whole reasoning: MP-006 and MP-044 distinguish knowledge of individual parts
from knowledge of their relationships. Whole-level evidence determines a
part-specific answer only when that answer is constant across the whole-states
consistent with the evidence. Conversely, retaining identical parts can leave a
changed dependency undetected.

Identity: CL-00026, CL-00310, and MP-043 distinguish objects, occurrences,
revisions, content digests, and history. The archived identifier design also
separates relational, lexical, semantic, and clarity metadata. Chained and Change
identifier terminology remains unresolved. These contracts do not establish
universal collision-freedom or prove an interpretation merely by assigning an ID.

Revision: MP-034 and MP-035 show why sufficient information for a current question
may be insufficient for a later update. Within the stated abstraction model,
equal summaries must remain equal after each permitted action for the abstract
update to be well-defined. Otherwise the missing distinction must be recovered
or the update withheld.

Navigation: Sphere of Webs models overlapping relationships while keeping identity
separate from viewpoint, spatial proximity, and authority. Navigation geometry
is an organizational design, not evidence for a mathematical claim.

Organizational experiments: C. elegans-inspired work supplies candidate mechanisms
for directional transport, reverse lookup, boundary coordination, and recovery.
Biological inspiration does not establish biological validity or transfer proof
status to an information system. Historical results have not been rerun here.

State transitions: the standalone Stairs of State specification is not available
in this recovered corpus. Related transition results may guide a future study,
but they are not substitutes for that missing source.

Learning through experiments: MP-067 through MP-076 examine accessible evidence
and experiment selection. MP-068's finite elimination argument requires the
actual case to be included in a finite candidate model and an available lawful
distinguishing experiment at each unresolved stage. Under those assumptions,
at most N-1 eliminations are needed to determine an answer class for N candidates.
This does not establish that the candidate model is complete or that suitable
experiments are easy, inexpensive, safe, or available in practice.

4. PROPOSED REQUIREMENTS
Stable identity separates objects from representations and interpretations.
Controlled revision records and checks changes without losing their context.
Reflective evaluation distinguishes intention, observation, inference, and failure.
Traceable execution specifies reasons, conditional successors, and recovery.
Relational context preserves dependencies and part-whole associations.
Explicit interpretation records scope, assumptions, meaning, and uncertainty.
Criteria-based grouping supports declared membership, overlap, and exceptions.
Versioned shared language supports consistent interpretation across boundaries.

REQUIREMENT_MAP.json associates these objectives with their earlier claim IDs.
The requirements remain proposals. No deployed autonomous implementation,
universal synchronization, grouping-convergence result, or human benefit follows
from the terminology alone.

5. EVIDENCE AND QUALIFICATIONS
The recovered corpus includes model-scoped mathematical arguments, definitions,
formal frameworks, corollaries, proposed applications, hypotheses, and historical
experimental reports. Their statuses are not interchangeable.

The claim index retains historical labels and inherited review categories.
PROOF_QUALIFICATIONS.txt identifies limitations involving decoder domains,
randomized safety, retained observations, jointly executable tests, and the
difference between diagnostic access and guessed-answer accuracy. This editorial
summary does not alter those qualifications or perform a new proof audit.

Relevant deductions include the compatibility of stable identity with revised
classification, the distinction between shared orientation and identical views,
and the need for explicit meaning alongside shared syntax. Internal evaluation
can expose a gap without providing independent corroboration.

The practical effects of better shared context, adaptive grouping, and common
record formats remain hypotheses requiring appropriate experiments.

6. GROUPING AND SHARED INTERPRETATION
Membership must identify its criterion, scope, evidence, and revision. An object
may belong to several groups, and an unknown relationship need not imply exclusion.

Pairwise compatibility alone need not satisfy a whole-group requirement. The
sets {a,b}, {b,c}, and {a,c} intersect pairwise but have no common element across
all three. This refutes substituting pairwise overlap for a shared-choice
requirement; it does not refute every possible grouping method.

A shared record format should identify objects, revisions, published statements,
claim types, assumptions, evidence, system revisions, relations, membership,
unknowns, conditional actions, and recovery references. Private sources remain
access-controlled; preserving provenance does not require publishing raw notes.
A new axiom or definition set creates a new context for reassessing earlier proofs.

7. NEXT EXPERIMENT
The example in ../examples/SMALLEST_WORKED_EXAMPLE.txt holds parts A and B fixed
while varying whether A depends on B. A parts-only view cannot distinguish the
two required answers; an accurate dependency record can within this fixed model.
Missing, contradictory, or stale evidence must remain explicit.

../NEXT_STEP.md describes the proposed comparison and outcome-dependent actions.
The experiment has not been executed by this editorial revision.

8. SOURCE AVAILABILITY

The proof-ledger capture contains 1,000 of its reported 1,282 lines; MP-045 is
incomplete. The standalone Stairs of State specification and a fully specified
grouping policy remain unavailable in this corpus. A retrieval gap is not proof
that the source does not exist.

This repository provides public summaries and indexes, not the complete archival
corpus. See ../provenance/SOURCE_IMPORT.json and ../provenance/PUBLIC_EXPORT.md
for source dispositions. Initial archival receipts describe their original
snapshot and do not certify the bytes of later edited summaries.
