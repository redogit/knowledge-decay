Epistemic Mereology / MP-006 and MP-044
The whole-to-part transfer criterion says that whole-level evidence supports a
part-specific answer exactly when that answer is constant across all whole-states
consistent with the evidence. The complementary counterexample retains every
part but changes a relation. Identical parts then require different answers.
Practical lesson: preserve the meaningful relationships, not just an inventory.
Source: S1.2; explicit short proofs reviewed in this record.

  Boundary: Identity-policy requirement; no universal collision-freedom established.

Self-updating
  Relevant earlier work: MP-013, MP-034, MP-035.
  Proposed application: Propose a versioned update; validate the complete successor; retain predecessor and rollback.
  Boundary: Conditional safety; sound verifier and correct state abstraction are not automatic.

Self-reflecting
  Relevant earlier work: MP-033, MP-043, MP-066.

Self-Pathing
  Relevant earlier work: MP-004, MP-069, MP-070.
  Proposed application: Every attempted step names its reason, conditional successors, stop condition and usable recovery route.
  Boundary: A predetermined transition rule does not predetermine an unknown experimental result.

  Relevant earlier work: MP-006, MP-044, MP-065.
  Proposed application: Maintain typed part-of, depends-on, contradicts and interpretation relations to a referenced whole-state.
  Boundary: Whole/part knowledge transfer is target-relative; all relations are not known by default.

Strict clarity
  Relevant earlier work: MP-001, CL-01033, CL-01034.
  Proposed application: Specify target, scope, assumptions, original wording, version, and what remains unknown.
  Boundary: Explicit uncertainty is compatible with strict clarity.

Move toward in-groups and away from out-groups
  Relevant earlier work: MP-014, MP-036, MP-038.
  Proposed application: Use declared compatibility constraints; preserve nonmembers, overlapping memberships, and unresolved relations.
  Boundary: Grouping motion is a hypothesis, not an established convergence theorem; pairwise compatibility is insufficient.

  Relevant earlier work: CL-00313, MP-059, MP-066.
  Proposed application: Shared typed envelope with versioned meanings and source-preserving translations.

6. GROUPING NEEDS A DIFFERENT TEST FROM IDENTITY

The Sphere already allows one object in several webs. Moving an object does not
change its identity, and an object outside a current group can remain relevant.
A useful strictness is: state the membership criterion, scope, evidence and revision.
Unknown membership is not automatic exclusion. Disagreement can be information.

Pairwise compatibility can be insufficient for one shared group obligation.
For example, the allowed choices {a,b}, {b,c}, and {a,c} overlap pairwise but have
no choice common to all three. This does not refute every possible grouping rule;
it refutes substituting pairwise agreement for the stated whole-group requirement.
