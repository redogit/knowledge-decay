# Publication policy

## Public writing

Public material should communicate the project's purpose, concepts, methods, limitations, and results in clear, audience-appropriate language. Working conversations are inputs to that writing process, not text to publish unchanged.

Preserve the intended meaning while improving structure and terminology. Remove personal commentary, conversational corrections, emotional disclosures, and incidental details that do not belong in a research document. Do not attribute polished wording as a verbatim quotation.

## Technical fidelity

Retain necessary identifiers, definitions, assumptions, and evidence qualifications. Where the intended meaning is unresolved, document the alternatives rather than silently choosing one. Editing must not turn an intuition into a theorem, a source label into independent verification, or a proposed capability into an implemented feature.

## Source custody

Keep archival source material and public summaries distinct. Permission to access or preserve a source is not permission to publish it. Private material requires explicit authorization for publication, including publication in issues, review discussions, commit messages, fixtures, or logs.

Source references may identify provenance and availability without exposing private text. Public readers must be told when the underlying evidence is not publicly available; a summary or digest is not a substitute for that evidence.

## Review

Before publication, check the text for personal material, unsupported claims, ambiguous terminology, broken references, and misleading source-status descriptions. Apply this review to the entire change, not only the README.

Updates to the current branch are editorial revisions, not a guarantee of erasure from earlier commits, caches, or external copies. Destructive history operations require separate authorization.
